# Closing Time — Game Concept

## Elevator pitch

Run a strange overnight roadside shop: keep the shelves stocked, serve people the right thing, and notice which customers are carrying trouble into the night.

## Core fantasy

You are the only person awake at a small shop on a dark road. The comfort comes from making the place work: tidy shelves, warm food, familiar routines. The tension comes from realizing the customers are not random.

## Pillars

1. **Hands-on shop work:** the player physically moves between shelves and the register to fulfill requests.
2. **Small meaningful decisions:** stock, questions, and service choices change cash, stock, trust, clues, and dawn outcomes.
3. **Cozy mystery:** the shop routine is calm and readable, while customer details gradually become strange.
4. **Compact place, strong atmosphere:** one shop should feel like a safe little island on the road.

## Anti-pillars

- No dialogue-only customer encounters
- No open world
- No multiplayer
- No crafting or farming
- No spreadsheet economy
- No giant dialogue tree
- No question that exists only to advance text

## Core loop

### Five-minute loop

1. Restock cold, warm, and odd shelves.
2. Call the next customer at the register.
3. Read their request and observe their tell.
4. Ask one question or skip it.
5. Walk to a shelf and take an item.
6. Return to the register and serve them.
7. See the result: cash, stock, trust, and a clue change.
8. Repeat until dawn.

### Customer interaction rules

- Every customer requests one shelf category: Cold, Warm, or Odd.
- Taking an item removes one unit from that shelf and places it in the player's hands.
- Serving with the requested category earns money and trust.
- Serving the wrong category loses trust and may reveal a different clue.
- Serving with empty hands is refused and costs time/trust.
- A question must reveal the requested category, improve trust, or expose a clue. Otherwise it does not belong in the game.

## MVP slice

One shop, one 8–12 minute shift, three customers, three shelf categories, six stock units, one useful question per customer, one mystery thread, and a dawn summary.

## Cheap Steam target

The first commercial test is not content volume. It is whether players enjoy the physical rhythm of stocking and serving enough to want one more customer, while the mystery gives them a reason to pay attention.
